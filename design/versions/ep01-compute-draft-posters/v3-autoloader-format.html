Databricks

What you’re renting, when it stops,
and where the money goes
Databricks doesn’t run your code — your own cloud account does. Every notebook, job and query runs on machines rented by the second. Which kind you pick is the biggest single lever on the bill, and most teams never actually choose.
It runs across two accounts
Control plane
Databricks’ account
  • The web UI and notebooks
  • The job scheduler
  • Table metadata
↓
Data plane
Your cloud account
  • Machines start here
  • Your storage — data never leaves
  • Your IAM and networking
Which is why you’re billed twice:
DBUs to Databricks, machines to your cloud.
Four ways to rent — pick by workload, not habit
Always-on
Exploring, building, sharing with the team
Stops whensomeone stops it
Per-job
A nightly report, a scheduled model run
Stops whenthe job finishes
Serverless
Short, bursty work you can’t predict
Stops whenthe request ends
SQL warehouse
Dashboards and everyday SQL questions
Stops whenit goes idle
How the bill is built
1
You start compute. Machines launch in your cloud account.
2
Your cloud provider bills you for those machines, by the second.
3
Databricks bills you separately in DBUs — its own usage meter.
4
The DBU rate differs by compute type. Always-on costs the most.
5
Nothing stops on its own unless you configured it to.
The settings that matter
# a job cluster — and why each line is there
spark_version: "16.4.x-scala2.12"  # LTS: won't shift
autoscale: { min_workers: 1, max_workers: 4 }

aws_attributes:
  availability: "SPOT"
  # workers cheap — and interruptible
  first_on_demand: 1
  # driver protected: lose it, lose the run

policy_id: "<admin-issued>"
# caps the size, forces the cost tags
Where the money actually leaks
Always-on, left running overnight
Oversized clusters “to be safe”
On-demand where spot was fine
Paid accelerators on Python-bound work
No tags, so nobody can attribute it
The rule that runs through every episode
Every setting here is buying you something at a price.
Spot buys a discount and costs you retries. LTS buys stability and costs you new features. If you can’t say what a setting bought you, you didn’t choose it — you inherited it.
